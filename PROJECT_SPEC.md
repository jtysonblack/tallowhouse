### Brand Identity

**Name:** Tallow House

**Tagline:** "Pure Smashburgers. The way it should be." (Primary) / "One Smashburger, One Way, The Way." / "The Best Burger Ever." / "Join the Family."

**Additional copy from social media:** "Twice as nice." / "Slinging smashburgers and fries for lunch and late night." / "We're only serving up the GOAT." / "Bringing Cleveland's favorite burger to Chattanooga." / "8PM–SELLOUT" (timing language — creates urgency)

**Positioning:** Cleveland, TN's cult-following smash burger brand — raw, gritty, community-first, with the energy of a streetwear drop and the soul of a neighborhood cookout. Pure ingredients, no seed oils, sourdough buns baked daily, hand-cut tallow fries. This is a health-forward fast-casual spot that never talks like one.

**Owner:** Nathan Zakkas — Founder. Public-facing, active on social media, featured in local press (Local 3 News "3 In Your Town" segment, Mix 104.1 radio interview). Hands-on operator who works the grill.

**Tone:** Bold, direct, confident. Never corporate. Talks like a friend who's dead serious about burgers. Uses short, punchy copy. Leans into street culture — car meets, hip-hop nights, community give-backs. The voice swings between hype-man energy ("THE BEST BURGER EVER") and earnest community pride ("Join the Family"). Copy should feel hand-stamped, not typeset.

**Primary accent color:** Sage green (#4D7158 – #5E8668 range). This is the brand's signature — it's on the walls, the merch, the aprons, everywhere in their photography.

**EST:** 2026 — use as a brand badge element. Area code 423 is also used as a brand mark. Both appear on merch: "TALLOW HOUSE EST 423 2026".

### Purpose

**What the site does:** Showcases Tallow House's brand, culture, and menu. Drives foot traffic to the physical location. Pushes visitors to follow social media for real-time updates (drops, events, hours changes). Accepts job applications.

**Primary conversion:** Drive visitors to follow on social media (Instagram is the primary communication channel — that's free for the business and where the community lives)

**Secondary conversion:** Job applications (grill cook and team positions → routes to Google Sheet or email)

**Audience:** Cleveland, TN locals and Chattanooga-area food enthusiasts. Ages 18–40 skewing younger. People who follow food culture on social media, attend car meets, value authenticity over polish. The owner explicitly wants to "compete with guys out in L.A." — the audience includes the Instagram food-culture crowd who discovers spots through social feeds. Social media imagery confirms: young, diverse crowd of 20+ people in storefront photos.

**Primary objections:**

- "Is it worth the drive?" — For Chattanooga visitors especially, the food photography and social proof need to make the case.
- "Are they open right now?" — Hours and location need to be immediately findable. They have non-standard hours (Mon–Thu 4PM–9PM, Fri–Sat 11AM–4PM) that change.

**Language they use to describe their problem:**
- Owner: "I'm building a brand, not just a burger place"
- Owner: "I want people to feel like they're part of something"
- Owner: "We're not doing online orders — come see us"
- Community: The Drop Initiative, "Join the Family"

**Proof points available:**
- Stats: None formal — but cult following evident from social engagement. Lines out the door and into the parking lot since opening. Drawing visitors from multiple states.
- Collaborations: Bless Yer Heart (233 E MLK Blvd, Chattanooga), Hoptown Beer Bar (1617 Rossville Ave, Chattanooga Southside) — legitimate Chattanooga food scene credibility. These are real multi-city brand partnerships, not just catering gigs.
- Testimonials: Pull from social media comments/reviews (owner mentioned reviews as important for the site). TripAdvisor 5.0/5 stars. Multiple TikTok food reviewers have covered the spot with glowing reviews.
- Community: The Drop Initiative (free burgers + anti-drug awareness events partnered with ATS The Bridge / Awareness Through Students, held at Old Woolen Mill), car meets, DJ nights — this is a brand that gives back
- Media coverage: Local 3 News "3 In Your Town" feature (reporter Paul Shahen), Mix 104.1 radio interview with Nathan. Multiple TikTok food blogger reviews. Professional photography library is extensive and high-quality.
- Ingredient story: Sourdough baked daily, tallow fries, no seed oils, organic beverages, reverse osmosis water, grassfed beef — these are real differentiators that resonate with the health-conscious food crowd without making the brand feel "health food"

**Traffic sources:** Instagram bio link (primary), word of mouth, Google search for "smash burgers Cleveland TN", potential Google Maps listing

The site needs to match or exceed that energy — if it feels more "corporate" than the Instagram, it fails. Some will arrive cold from Google and need the full brand experience from scratch.

### Design System Expectations

**A complete design token system already exists:** `tallow-house-tokens.css`

The build should use this token file directly. It includes:

- **Color primitives:** 5 scales (Sage, Amber, Char, Cream, Rust) with 11 stops each, extracted from professional photography via computational color analysis
- **Semantic tokens:** Full mapping for backgrounds, text, borders, buttons, focus states, status colors — in both light and dark themes
- **Typography system:** Teko (display/headings — bold, condensed, matches hand-painted chalkboard + merch type) + Work Sans (body — warm, readable, humanist sans-serif). Scale from `--text-xs` through `--text-5xl` with defined line heights and letter spacing.
- **Spacing scale:** 4px base unit, tokens from `--space-1` (4px) through `--space-16` (64px)
- **Border radii:** Sharp by default (2–4px) — "this isn't a soft brand"
- **Shadows:** Subtle, warm-toned shadows using char tones
- **Motion:** Defined easing curves and duration tokens
- **Film grain overlay:** SVG feTurbulence-based grain texture with mix-blend-mode — this is a signature design element that matches the social media photography aesthetic
- **Dark theme ("Chalkboard Mode"):** Uses char-900 as ground color. This is the most authentically Tallow House surface — their actual restaurant has chalkboard menus and dark interior surfaces. Full token remapping for dark theme via both `prefers-color-scheme` media query and `data-theme="dark"` attribute.

**The token file is the source of truth.** Do not create a new design system. Import and use `tallow-house-tokens.css` as-is. Build components that reference these semantic tokens.

A visual reference for the token system exists at `tallow-house-design-system.html` in the same directory.

### Design Direction

**Aesthetic intent:** Raw, gritty, community. GREEN-FORWARD — sage green is the dominant surface color, not black. The site should feel like the restaurant's Instagram come to life as a full experience — not cleaner, not more polished, but deeper. The grainy film-quality photography, the bold block typography, the unapologetic confidence. It should feel like a streetwear brand that happens to make the best smash burgers you've ever had. Film grain overlay, tight punchy copy in Teko, photography doing the heavy lifting. This is a working man's burger — the design should feel hands-on, earned, and real.

**CRITICAL COLOR DIRECTION:** "Gritty" means TEXTURE and GRAIN, not dark/black color palette. The building is green. The aprons are green. The merch is green. The social media backgrounds are green. Sage green is the PRIMARY color of this brand and should be the PRIMARY color of the site. Think green colorwash — the grain/gritty filter goes OVER green surfaces, not over black surfaces. Literally none of their social media uses flat black. Do NOT default to a dark/black theme. Use the grain SVG overlay on sage green grounds, cream grounds, and photography — that's what creates the gritty feel, not darkness.

#### Social Media Visual Analysis (PRIMARY design source — carries more weight than transcript)

14 Instagram/Facebook posts were analyzed in detail. These are THE definitive reference for the site's visual direction. Key findings:

**Color hierarchy across social content — GREEN DOMINATES:**

1. **Sage green ground + cream type = THE PRIMARY MODE (70%+ of social content).** Used for announcements, hours updates, hiring posts, community events, merch. Sage green gradient background (sometimes darkening at edges), cream/off-white typography. THIS IS THE DEFAULT FOR THE SITE. Green walls, green aprons, green ink on merch, green backgrounds on posts — green is Tallow House.

2. **Dark ground + amber/gold type = SECONDARY accent mode (minority of social content).** Used for a few collab menus and special event flyers. Even these are not flat black — they have heavy grain texture. This is an accent, NOT the site's foundation. Use sparingly for specific sections (an event callout, a dramatic food photo moment), never as the site's overall feel.

**Typography — three voices, not one:**

The brand uses more than just bold condensed sans-serif. Social media reveals a deliberate three-voice type system:

1. **Heavy condensed sans-serif** (Teko 600–700 equivalent) — the primary voice. ALL CAPS. Headlines, menu items, announcements, the loudest content. This is what screams "Tallow House."
2. **Script/cursive accent** — used sparingly for warmth and personality. Appears in: "takeover!", "New Tallow House" (on hours post), cocktail names (TH Old Fashioned, Tallow Back, Tallow Tai), hand-circled CTAs on the hiring post. This voice humanizes the bold type. Consider a handwritten or script Google Font for these accent moments (the token system's display face is Teko; a complementary script can be added as a third face for accent use only).
3. **Serif or upright sans-serif** — appears in smaller body/subtitle copy on some posts ("Bringing Cleveland's favorite burger to Chattanooga." uses a thinner serif). Work Sans handles this role in the token system.

**Film grain is on EVERYTHING:**
Photography, flyer backgrounds, even t-shirt mockup backgrounds have visible grain overlay. The neon OPEN sign shot is the purest example: heavy film grain, warm amber/orange tones, bokeh blur showing interior with people. Classic diner atmosphere. The grain SVG filter in the token system replicates this and should be applied as a persistent atmospheric layer, not an occasional effect.

**Design patterns to replicate on the site:**

- **Numbered grid (01–06):** The Bless Yer Heart collab menu uses a numbered grid layout for menu items — each item gets a number, a name, and a price in a clean grid. This is a strong pattern for the Menu page.
- **Bordered badge boxes:** Three side-by-side badge/pill elements reading "PURE SMASHBURGERS" / "SOURDOUGH BUNS BAKED DAILY" / "HANDCUT TALLOW FRIES" — these are the brand pillars and appear as bordered boxes on social posts. Replicate as a component.
- **Green-bordered vertical photo strip:** Used on the hiring flyer — a tall, bordered frame containing stacked kitchen action photos. Good pattern for vertical content blocks.
- **Green starburst/explosion shape:** Used as a background element for emphasis (visible on the hours announcement post containing the schedule). Use sparingly as a graphic accent.
- **Hand-drawn circle emphasis:** Script-style circled text for CTAs — hand-drawn, imperfect, personal. This could translate to a hover state or CTA treatment on the site.
- **Framed photography:** Some posts use bordered/framed photo treatments (thin rule borders around photos within the composition). The Hoptown collab uses a thin white serif title inside a bordered frame.

**Key images for the site (from social media analysis):**

- **Storefront at night:** THE hero image. Sage green painted facade, large circular mascot logo sign lit by spotlights, neon OPEN sign in window, "4602" address visible, crowd of 20+ people outside. This single image communicates everything: the brand color, the logo, the community, the energy, the location.
- **Flat-top grill action:** ~12 smash patties with melting American cheese, black-gloved hand with metal spatula. Very dark, heavy film grain. This is the visceral "we make this with our hands" shot.
- **Neon OPEN sign through window:** Heavy grain, warm amber tones, bokeh blur showing interior activity. Atmospheric — conveys the lived-in, warm diner feeling.
- **Merch t-shirt (cream tee, sage green ink):** Shows the illustrated building at 4602, sports cars (Corvette, Datsun Z), motorcycle, mascot logo. "THE BEST BURGER EVER" in heavy condensed/distressed type. "TALLOW HOUSE EST 423 2026" at bottom. This is the merch aesthetic the site should reference.

**Mascot / Logo details (from social media analysis):**

The logo IS the mascot in a circle. It is the PRIMARY and seemingly ONLY brand mark. Seen on: the building signage (large, lit by spotlights), all merch, all flyers, all social posts. Details: muscular cartoon man with dark hair, mustache, green apron over white t-shirt, flexing right bicep, holding burger in left hand. Friendly/confident expression. "TALLOW HOUSE" arches over the top of the circle in sans-serif caps. This is not a secondary brand element — it is THE brand mark. The site header should feature this mark alongside or instead of a text-only logo once the asset is extracted.

**Open to:**
- Video background (owner has sizzle video and drone footage — this is expected on the homepage)
- Motion/animation that matches the energy (but tasteful — not gratuitous)
- Interactive elements that reward exploration
- Photography as the dominant content type — let the images do the talking
- Scroll-driven reveals that build the brand story
- Alternating green/cream sections within a page for visual rhythm, with dark sections used very sparingly for dramatic moments
- Script/handwritten accent font for select CTA moments and accent copy

**Avoid:**
- Corporate restaurant website templates. This is the #1 thing that would kill the brand.
- Soft, rounded, "friendly" design language. Sharp corners. Bold type. Confident spacing.
- Stock photography of any kind. Only use the professional photos provided.
- Light, airy, minimalist aesthetics. This brand is warm, textured, lived-in.
- Flat black or dark themes as the dominant palette. Green is the brand color — use it. "Gritty" = grain texture, not dark colors.
- Desaturated or muted palettes. The green should be confident and present, not washed out.
- Online ordering UI, "Order Now" buttons, delivery integrations. The owner is explicit: no online orders, no call-ins. Come to the restaurant.
- Calligraphy or "artisan" food branding cliches. NOTE: the brand DOES use script/cursive as an accent voice — that's different from calligraphy branding. The script is casual and hand-drawn, not elegant.
- Excessive whitespace or sparse layouts. The social media is dense with energy — the site should match.
- Using the mascot as a secondary/decorative element. It IS the logo. Treat it accordingly.

**Differentiation:** The film grain over bold green surfaces. The gritty texture as atmosphere. The community-first storytelling (Drop Initiative, car meets). The audacious confidence of a small-town burger spot that says "I want us to compete with guys out in L.A." and means it. The intentional simplicity of the menu presented as a feature, not a limitation. The site should make you feel like you found something before everyone else did.
